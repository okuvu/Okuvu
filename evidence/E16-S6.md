### 5.3 The tolerance, and why it is legitimate

Documented in `docs/RESTORE-RUNBOOK.md` §5. In short:

- **Migration-seeded registries and `_prisma_migrations`: exact.** They are written by migrations,
  never by runtime traffic; drift is corruption, not lag.
- **Schema shape: exact.** A snapshot copy reproduces indexes/constraints bit-for-bit; clock skew is
  not an explanation for a missing index.
- **Client/clinical tables: `scratch ≥ prod_at_SnapshotCreateTime` and `|scratch − prod_now| ≤
  writes_after_snapshot`.** This is the only honest source of a difference on a live system: the
  snapshot is a point in time and production keeps accepting writes. The comparison above is
  therefore not "equal or bust" by fiat — the expected delta is zero *because* beta currently holds
  no client data (only 10 seeded registry rows and 43 ledger rows), and the measured delta is zero
  on every one of the 38 tables.

Comparison instants differ by 58s (prod→run A) and 9m26s (prod→run B); no writes landed in either
window, which the zero deltas confirm rather than assume.

## 9. What is **not** verified here

1. **Restore onto / over production.** Only scratch restores were drilled. Point-in-time recovery
   onto the live instance, and the cutover step that would repoint `DATABASE_URL` at a restored
   instance, are not exercised — both are deliberate production changes, out of scope.
2. **Restoring with real client data.** Beta holds no clinical rows yet (`User` = 0). Exact-equality
   was therefore easy to achieve; the tolerance rule for a busy database (§5.3) is documented but
   has not been exercised against non-trivial data volume.
3. **Restore duration at production scale.** Measured at 20 GiB. The 6–10 minute figure will not
   hold for a much larger database; the runbook says to re-measure after any storage change.
4. **PITR (`restore-db-instance-to-point-in-time`).** Not measured at all. `LatestRestorableTime`
   was observed as 2026-10-28T19:45:26Z during the drill but no PITR restore was performed.
5. **Cross-region / cross-account recovery.** Only same-region restore from a snapshot in place. A
   total-region loss would need snapshot copies that do not currently exist.
6. **The application against the restored database.** The probe uses the `pg` client, not the api. I
   did not repoint a running api/worker at the scratch instance and take traffic — the counts prove
   the data is there, not that the app would serve correctly from it. (The known `sslmode=require`
   defect from E16-S4 is orthogonal but would apply.)
7. **Automated-backup deletion timing.** Confirmed the drill's automated snapshot was gone by the
   final listing, but not the exact moment RDS removed it.
8. **Alarms / event notifications.** No CloudWatch alarm fired on the restore, and none was expected
   to; I did not validate that monitoring would notice a real restore event.

Deliberately not touched: production instance (`evia-beta-postgres`) in any mutating way, the manual
snapshot `evia-beta-premigrate-202610281906`, the two production automated snapshots, the Terraform
state and `infrastructure/`, `plan/run-ledger.psv`, and the `develop` branch.
