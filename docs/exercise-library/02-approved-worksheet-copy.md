OKUVU LAUNCH WORKSHEETS — APPROVED WORDING v1

Approved: worksheets 1–16 and the shared app copy, approved by Dr. Shubhangi Goswami (clinical lead), 9 November 2026.

Use this wording exactly as written. Hinglish versions are written from this approved English text and need separate approval.

How to read each worksheet: where it sits in the app, who can open it, how demanding it is, rough time, a one-line note for therapists, then the screens in order. Each numbered step is one screen on the phone.

Who can open it: Self-serve = any client can open it. Assigned = only after a therapist assigns it. Co-created = filled together in session.

Demanding (high-load) worksheets are blocked when the client's latest feeling rating is above 5 out of 10, and the client is sent to grounding instead.

NOTICE & NAME

1. MOOD CHECK-IN (extends the existing one)

Self-serve · Light · 1 min · For therapists: tracks mood, trigger and intensity over time; its intensity rating also drives the safety gate on demanding worksheets.

The existing mood check-in stays as it is. These two screens are added after it.

Screen 1: "What was happening just before you felt this?"
Short answer, optional. Helper: "A place, a person, a thought, a notification, nothing in particular. All of these count." Example: "Got my internal marks back."

Screen 2: "How strong is this feeling right now?"
Slider 0–10. Ends: "0 = barely there", "10 = the strongest I've felt."

Closing line: "Noted. Noticing is a skill, and you just used it."

2. NAME THE FEELING

Self-serve · Light · 3 min · For therapists: emotion granularity and body awareness; useful early in therapy and before DBT skills.

Intro: "Sometimes everything just feels like 'bad' or 'off.' Putting a more exact name on a feeling can make it a little easier to hold."

Screen 1: "Pick the feeling closest to what's going on."
Feelings picker: starts with broad families (sad, scared, angry, ashamed, happy, calm, numb), then opens finer words (for example under scared: worried, panicky, unsafe, on edge). Client can pick up to three.

Screen 2: "How strong is each one?"
Slider 0–10 for each feeling picked.

Screen 3: "Where do you notice it in your body?"
Checklist: head, jaw, throat, chest, stomach, shoulders, hands, legs, nowhere I can tell. Helper: "There's no wrong answer. Some feelings don't show up in the body at all."

Screen 4: "If this feeling could say one sentence, what would it say?"
Short answer, optional. Example: "Don't mess this up."

Closing line: "You gave it a name. That's a step towards understanding it."

CALM MY BODY

These stay open at any feeling level. They are where clients are sent when a demanding worksheet is blocked.

3. 5-4-3-2-1 GROUNDING

Self-serve · Light · 3 min · For therapists: grounding through the senses for high distress, dissociation or panic.

Note for workspace: 5-4-3-2-1 grounding already exists with approved wording. Keep the existing version and add only the optional writing box for each sense, the "How are you feeling now?" slider, and the closing buttons below.

Intro: "When your mind is racing, your senses can bring you back to right here. Go slowly. You don't need to type anything unless you want to."

Screen 1: "Look around. Find 5 things you can see."
Optional short answer. Helper: "Small things count: a crack in the wall, the colour of your sleeve."

Screen 2: "Notice 4 things you can feel."
Optional short answer. Helper: "Your feet on the floor, the weight of your phone, the air on your skin."

Screen 3: "Listen for 3 things you can hear."
Optional short answer. Helper: "Near or far: a fan, traffic, your own breathing."

Screen 4: "Find 2 things you can smell."
Optional short answer. Helper: "If nothing stands out, notice the smell of your hands or your clothes."

Screen 5: "Notice 1 thing you can taste."
Optional short answer. Helper: "Even just the inside of your mouth, or a sip of water."

Screen 6: "How are you feeling now?"
Slider 0–10, compared with the rating before, if there was one.

Closing line: "You're here. Take whatever time you need before moving on." Buttons: "Open my Treasure Box" and "Done for now".

4. EXISTING BREATHING EXERCISES (four)

Already in the app: box breathing, physiological sigh, extended exhale, humming breath. They are only being moved into the Library, with no new wording. Each ends with the same "How are you feeling now?" slider (0–10) as grounding. Guided breathing is retired and stays hidden.

UNTANGLE MY THOUGHTS

All three are assigned only and demanding. If the client's latest feeling rating is above 5 out of 10, grounding opens instead.

5. THOUGHT RECORD

Assigned · Demanding · 10 min · For therapists: classic CBT thought record. The other way of seeing it is written by the client only; OKUVU never suggests one.

Intro: "Thoughts can feel like facts, especially when they show up fast. This is a way to slow one down and look at it from a few sides. Pick one moment from today or this week."

Screen 1: "What happened?"
Short answer. Helper: "Just the facts, like a camera would see it: where, when, who." Example: "My friend saw my message and didn't reply for five hours."

Screen 2: "What did you feel, and how strongly?"
Feelings picker with a 0–10 slider for each.

Screen 3: "What went through your mind?"
Long answer. Helper: "Write the thought the way it sounded in your head, even if it seems extreme." Example: "She's annoyed with me. I always say the wrong thing."

Screen 4: "Which thought feels the strongest?"
Short answer, plus slider "How much do you believe it right now?" 0–100%.

Screen 5: "What makes this thought feel true?"
List, client can add rows. Helper: "Things that actually happened, not guesses."

Screen 6: "What doesn't quite fit with this thought?"
List, client can add rows. Helper: "Anything that suggests it might not be the whole story. If you can't find anything yet, that's okay. Leave it and come back."

Screen 7: "Looking at both lists, is there another way to see this?"
Long answer, optional. Helper: "This doesn't have to be positive. It just has to be fair to all of what you wrote." No example is given, so the client's own view is not shaped.

Screen 8: "Now, how much do you believe the original thought?"
Slider 0–100%, then "And how strong is the feeling now?" slider 0–10.

Closing line: "Whatever you found, you looked at your own mind with care. That's the practice." Buttons: "Save to my Treasure Box" and "Done".

6. SPOT THE THINKING TRAP

Assigned · Demanding · 5 min · For therapists: names common thinking errors in plain language. Use after explaining thinking errors in session.

Intro: "Our minds have shortcuts. They're quick, but sometimes they take us somewhere unfair. See if any of these sound familiar."

Screen 1: "Write down a thought that's been bothering you."
Short answer.

Screen 2: "Does it fit any of these patterns?"
Checklist, each with a one-line description:
Mind reading: guessing what others think without checking.
Fortune telling: being sure something will go badly.
All-or-nothing: if it's not perfect, it's a failure.
Catastrophising: jumping to the worst possible outcome.
Should statements: strict rules about how I or others must be.
Labelling: calling myself a name instead of describing what happened.
Discounting the good: deciding positives don't count.
Emotional reasoning: I feel it, so it must be true.
None of these fit.

Screen 3: "What do you notice now that you've named it?"
Long answer, optional.

Closing line: "Spotting a pattern doesn't make the feeling wrong. It just gives you a little more room."

7. WORRY TREE

Assigned · Demanding · 5 min · For therapists: separates worries that need action from "what if" worries, then contains the rest with a set worry time.

Intro: "Some worries need action. Others just keep circling. This helps you sort which is which."

Screen 1: "What's the worry?"
Short answer. Example: "What if I don't get placed this year?"

Screen 2: "Is this about something happening now, or a 'what if' about later?"
Choice: "Something happening now" or "A 'what if'".

Screen 3: "Is there anything you can do about it?"
Choice: "Yes" or "Not right now".

Screen 4:
If yes: "What's one small step, and when could you do it?" Short answer plus optional date and time.
If not right now: "Park it for worry time." Pick a 15-minute slot later today or tomorrow. Helper: "When the worry shows up before then, remind yourself: 'I've got a time for this.' Then bring your attention back to what you're doing."

Screen 5: Worry time (opens at the chosen slot): "Look back at what you parked. Does it still feel as urgent? Is there anything you can do about it now?" Long answer, optional.

Closing line: "You don't have to solve everything at once."

DO WHAT MATTERS

8. SMALL THINGS PLANNER

Assigned · Light · 5 min to plan, then quick check-offs · For therapists: behavioural activation with achievement and enjoyment ratings. Light so it stays open on low-mood days, when it matters most.

Intro: "When you're low, waiting to feel like doing something can take forever. Sometimes doing a small thing comes first, and the feeling follows a little. Plan a few small things. Tiny is fine."

Screen 1: "Plan up to three small things for the next few days."
Table with two columns: "What" and "When" (date and time). Helper: "Mix it up if you can: one thing you enjoy, one thing that gets something done, one thing that connects you with someone." Examples: "Walk to the chai stall", "Reply to one pending email", "Send a meme to my cousin."

Screen 2 (opens from the Today card after each one): "Did you do it?"
Choice: "Yes", "Partly" or "Not this time".

Screen 3:
If yes or partly: "How much did it feel like an achievement?" Slider 0–10. "How much did you enjoy it?" Slider 0–10.
If not this time: "What got in the way?" Short answer, optional. Helper: "No judgement. This helps you and your therapist plan better."

Closing line: "Every small thing counts, including the ones that didn't happen. You're learning what works for you."

9. VALUES COMPASS

Assigned · Light · 10 min · For therapists: ACT values work across life areas, ending in one small committed step.

Intro: "Goals are things you finish. Values are directions you keep walking in, like being a caring friend or someone who keeps learning. This helps you notice what matters to you."

Screen 1: "Which areas of life matter most to you right now?"
Checklist, pick up to three: family, friendships, love and relationships, studies or work, health and body, fun and rest, community and giving back, growth and learning, faith or spirituality.

Screen 2: "For each area: what kind of person do you want to be there?"
Short answer for each area. Helper: "Describe how you want to act, not what you want to get." Example for friendships: "Someone who shows up, even when it's inconvenient."

Screen 3: "How close are you living to that right now?"
Slider 0–10 for each area. Helper: "This isn't a score of how good you are. It just shows where you'd like to move."

Screen 4: "Pick one area. What's one small thing you could do this week that points in that direction?"
Short answer plus optional date.

Closing line: "You don't need to feel ready to take a step in a direction you care about." Buttons: "Save to my Treasure Box" and "Done".

PEOPLE & BOUNDARIES

10. PLAN A HARD CONVERSATION

Assigned · Light · 10 min · For therapists: DBT interpersonal effectiveness (DEAR MAN structure), in plain language. The skill name appears only in a small "Based on" line, so it stays approachable.

Intro: "Asking for something, or saying no, is hard, especially with family or people you care about. Planning what you want to say can make it feel less overwhelming. You choose whether and when to use it."

Screen 1: "Who is this conversation with, and what's it about?"
Short answer. Example: "My dad, about taking a gap semester."

Screen 2: "What do you want to come out of it?"
Short answer. Helper: "Be as clear as you can, for example 'I want them to hear my reasons' or 'I want to say no to the trip.'"

Screen 3: Describe: "What's the situation, just the facts?"
Short answer.

Screen 4: Express: "How do you feel about it? Try starting with 'I feel…'"
Short answer.

Screen 5: Ask: "What exactly are you asking for, or saying no to?"
Short answer.

Screen 6: Reinforce: "Why might this be good for them too, or for your relationship?"
Short answer, optional.

Screen 7: Stay on track: "If the conversation drifts or gets heated, what's one line you can come back to?"
Short answer. Example: "I hear you. I'd still like us to think about this."

Screen 8: Stay steady: "How do you want to sound: calm, firm, kind?"
Checklist.

Screen 9: Be open: "What could you accept if you can't get everything you want?"
Short answer, optional.

Screen 10: "Here's your plan." A one-screen summary of screens 3 to 9 that the client can read before the conversation.

Closing line: "You don't control how they respond. You can control how clearly you show up." Buttons: "Save to my Treasure Box" and "Done".

BE KINDER TO ME

11. A LETTER FROM A KIND FRIEND

Assigned · Demanding · 10 min · For therapists: compassion-focused letter writing. Marked demanding because it can stir up strong shame or grief, so it is better done when the client is steadier.

Intro: "Most of us talk to ourselves in ways we'd never talk to a friend. This is a chance to try a different voice."

Screen 1: "What's something you've been hard on yourself about lately?"
Long answer. Helper: "Write it the way your inner critic says it."

Screen 2: "Picture someone who cares about you without conditions. It can be a real person, someone you've imagined, or a wiser, kinder version of you. Who are you picturing?"
Short answer, optional.

Screen 3: "Write to yourself as they would."
Long answer. Helper questions appear one at a time, and the client can skip any: "What would they understand about why this is hard?", "What would they want you to know?", "What would they hope for you?"

Screen 4: "Read your letter back. How does it feel to read it?"
Short answer, optional. Helper: "If it feels awkward or untrue, that's very common. Kindness towards yourself can take practice."

Closing line: "You can come back to this letter whenever you need it." Buttons: "Save to my Treasure Box" and "Done".

12. THREE GOOD THINGS

Self-serve · Light · 3 min · For therapists: positive psychology practice of savouring; low effort and suitable for any day.

Intro: "Our brains are good at remembering what went wrong. This is practice in noticing what went okay. Small and ordinary is perfect."

Screen 1: "What's one thing that went okay today?" Short answer. Example: "The auto driver didn't argue about the fare."
Screen 2: "And another?" Short answer.
Screen 3: "And one more?" Short answer.
Screen 4: "Pick one. What part did you play in it, even a small one?" Short answer, optional.

Closing line: "Noticed and kept." Button: "Save to my Treasure Box".

ENERGY & LIFE

13. ENERGY AUDIT

Self-serve · Light · 5 min · For therapists: burnout self-monitoring of what drains and what restores energy, ending in one small adjustment.

Intro: "Some things take more out of you than they give back. Others quietly fill you up. Let's look at your week."

Screen 1: "What's been draining your energy this week?"
List, client can add rows, each with a slider "How much?" 0–10. Examples: "Back-to-back classes", "Family WhatsApp group", "Scrolling till 2 am."

Screen 2: "What's been giving you energy, even a little?"
List, client can add rows, each with a slider 0–10. Examples: "Evening walk", "Cooking with my roommate", "Cricket on Sunday."

Screen 3: "Looking at both lists, what do you notice?"
Long answer, optional.

Screen 4: "Is there one drain you could shrink a little, or one source you could add a bit more of this week?"
Short answer, optional. Helper: "Tiny changes count. If nothing feels possible right now, that's useful to know too."

Closing line: "Knowing where your energy goes is the first step to protecting it."

BEFORE & AFTER SESSION

14. WHAT I WANT TO BRING TO SESSION

Self-serve · Light · 3 min · For therapists: client-led agenda setting. If shared, it appears in session preparation.

Intro: "Sessions can go by fast, and things slip your mind. Jot down what you'd like to talk about, so it's ready when you need it."

Screen 1: "What's been on your mind since last session?"
Long answer.

Screen 2: "Is there anything you've been finding hard to say out loud?"
Long answer, optional. Helper: "You can write it here first. You decide whether to share it."

Screen 3: "If you could leave next session with one thing, what would it be?"
Short answer, optional. Examples: "A plan for exam week", "Just feeling heard."

Screen 4: "Share with your therapist before the session?"
Choice: "Share" or "Keep private". Both buttons are the same size and neither is chosen in advance.

Closing line: "Ready when you are."

15. POST-SESSION TAKEAWAY

Self-serve · Light · 3 min · For therapists: holds on to learning between sessions; the main way insights from therapy reach the Treasure Box.

Intro: "Right after a session is a good time to catch what stood out, before the day carries on."

Screen 1: "What's one thing you want to remember from today?"
Long answer. Example: "Feeling anxious doesn't mean something bad is about to happen."

Screen 2: "Was there anything you'd like to try before next time?"
Short answer, optional.

Screen 3: "Anything that didn't sit right, or that you'd like to come back to?"
Short answer, optional. Helper: "It's okay to disagree with something from session. That's worth bringing up next time."

Screen 4: "Save the first answer to your Treasure Box?"
Choice: "Save it" or "Not now". If saved, the client can choose an "Open when…" collection.

Closing line: "Kept safe for whenever you need it."

SAFETY

16. MY SAFETY PLAN

Co-created · Filled in during session from the portal · Client can view it at any time · For therapists: follows the standard six-part structure of collaborative safety planning. Written in the client's own words during session; only the therapist can edit it. Reachable in one tap from the crisis screen.

Title the client sees: "My plan for hard moments"

Intro (client view): "You made this with your therapist for times when things feel too much. It's here whenever you need it."

Part 1: "Signs a hard moment might be coming"
List. Question for the therapist to ask in session: "What thoughts, feelings, situations or behaviours tell you things are getting difficult?"

Part 2: "Things I can do on my own to get through it"
List. Question: "What helps you, even a little, when you're by yourself?" Each item can link to an OKUVU exercise, for example 5-4-3-2-1 grounding or a breathing exercise.

Part 3: "People and places that help take my mind off it"
List. Question: "Who or where can you be around, without having to talk about how you feel?"

Part 4: "People I can ask for help"
List with name and phone number, each with a one-tap call button. Question: "Who could you tell that you're struggling?"

Part 5: "Professional and crisis support"
The therapist's contact details, entered by the therapist, plus the verified helpline list. The helpline list comes from the app's central helpline list and must be checked before every release. It is not typed into each plan.

Part 6: "Making my surroundings safer"
List. Question: "What can you do to make your space safer during a hard moment?" Worded in session with the client.

Part 7: "One reason that matters to me"
Short answer, optional. Question: "What's something worth holding on to?"

No closing line. The plan always ends with a fixed "Get help now" button that opens the crisis screen.
